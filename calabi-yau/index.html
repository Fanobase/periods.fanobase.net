Fano fourfolds: quantum periods

regularized quantum periods and their geometric constructions

Calabi–Yau threefold links

The catalogue compares every recorded Fano period with 561 fourth-order operators in the Mainz Calabi–Yau differential operator database (CYDB). The captured records have maximally unipotent monodromy at the origin.

46 periods have an operator identity and 0 have coefficient agreement only. Across all 955 full and partial records, 909 have no match under this search and 0 have insufficient data. The main table retains the actual precision of every record.

The geometric connection

If a smooth Fano fourfold $X$ has a smooth anticanonical divisor $Y$, adjunction gives $K_Y=0$; the usual cohomology vanishings make $Y$ a Calabi–Yau threefold. This applies to higher Fano index as well as index one. Quantum Lefschetz relates the genus-zero systems, subject to its hypotheses and the required normalization. The relation between the regularized Fano equation and an anticanonical CY3 equation is discussed in Coates–Kasprzyk–Prince, Remark 3.3.

A fourth-order CYDB search is useful for one-parameter CY3 systems. Anticanonical sections of fourfolds with higher Picard rank can lead to higher-order one-variable specializations or to systems in several variables. A missing fourth-order match is therefore useful search information, not an obstruction to the anticanonical construction.

Exactly what is compared

Let $A(x)=\sum a_nx^n$ be the CYDB holomorphic solution normalized by $a_0=1$. We test $\widehat G_X(t)=(1+cst^p)^{-1}A(st^p/(1+cst^p))$, with $c=0$ or $c=a_1$, nonzero rational $s$, and integer $1\leq p\leq 5$. The centered choice $c=a_1$ removes the linear term in the primitive variable. The recorded power is a coordinate choice; it does not determine the geometric Fano index.

Every available Fano coefficient must agree exactly, including zero coefficients between powers of $t^p$. At least 16 coefficients in that primitive variable are required. CYDB seed terms are checked against the source operator and extended by its exact recurrence. Comparison lengths are recorded separately from the supplied seed lengths. Multiple matches are retained.

For example, the period of $\mathbf P^4$ is $A(t^5)$ for the quintic CY3 series $A(x)=\sum (5n)!/(n!)^5\,x^n$ (AESZ 1). The quintic Fano fourfold CKP(738) gives the same CYDB series with $p=1$, $s=1$, $c=120$. Both identities also hold at the level of their recorded differential operators. See the linked periods.

Evidence and geometric meaning

Operator identity means that, after the displayed pullback and gauge change, the full polynomial differential operators agree up to a nonzero rational function multiplying on the left. This is an exact algebraic comparison, independent of the number of matching coefficients. It is checked against captured fourth-order Fano operators; higher-order operators are not automatically replaced by guessed fourth-order factors.

Coefficients only means that all recorded coefficients agree but no such operator identity is attached. An operator identity with an unproved source operator does not prove its quantum interpretation. Each period page retains the independent Fano operator proof status.

Of the 46 operator identities, 42 use a Fano operator with a recorded exact derivation from a computed quantum connection. The remaining source operators retain their unproved status.

Neither status alone identifies a CY3 deformation family. The reported $H^3$, $c_2H$ and $c_3$ values and the source notes belong to the CYDB record. They must not be transferred to an anticanonical section without a geometric argument.
